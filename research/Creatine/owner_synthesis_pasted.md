## 1. Efficacy evidence base, by claim
- **Strength & power (with resistance training): Strong.** Multiple meta-analyses + 2017 ISSN position stand (2021 update context): creatine monohydrate reliably increases high-intensity capacity, strength, power with RT. Consistent in young adults; benefits also in older adults.
- **Lean muscle mass (with RT): Strong.** Meta-analyses ~1.1 kg additional LBM/fat-free mass vs placebo+RT (e.g., analysis of 35 studies). Training-dependent; creatine alone minimal. Across ages, often larger in males.
- **High-intensity repeated-effort (sprint/HIIT): Strong.** ISSN + reviews/metas: 10–20% improvements in high-intensity capacity, repeated sprint, anaerobic power. Clearest for phosphocreatine-dependent efforts.
- **Recovery: Moderate.** Reduced CK/soreness/inflammation markers, better force recovery (~10–21% some measures), enhanced glycogen restoration w/ carbs/protein. ISSN supports recovery + rehab. Fewer large metas; context-dependent.
- **Cognitive/brain: Moderate (context-specific) to Weak (general healthy adults).** Brain creatine +5–15%. Benefits in stressed states (sleep deprivation, hypoxia, mental fatigue) + low-baseline populations (vegetarians/vegans, older adults). Overall cognitive enhancement in unstressed healthy adults mixed/null in recent metas. Promising, not robustly established broadly.
- **Fat loss: Insufficient.** No credible primary evidence. Some secondary fat-mass reduction in older adults + RT (~0.5 kg extra, one meta). Matches internal reference.
- **"Energy" (acute stimulant): Insufficient.** Supports ATP/PCr, not an acute energizer.
- **Anti-aging: Weak/Preliminary.** Muscle/bone in older adults w/ training; broader claims lack strong human outcome data.
- **Mood: Weak/Preliminary.** Limited depression data (adjunctive); not established primary mood enhancer.

## 2. Effective dose ranges, by goal
- **Loading:** ~0.3 g/kg/day (~20 g/day, 4 doses) 5–7 days, then maintenance. Loading accelerates saturation but NOT necessary — 3–5 g/day for ~28 days reaches similar steady state.
- **Maintenance:** min effective ~3 g/day; typical 3–5 g/day (0.03–0.1 g/kg). Upper studied up to 10 g/day (larger/clinical); short-term loading up to ~30 g/day used safely.
- **By factor:** no major sex/training-status differences for physical goals; body-weight scaling sometimes used. Cognitive uses similar 3–5 g/day chronic or acute 0.2 g/kg in stressed states; higher acute (≥10 g) explored for brain. Vegetarians/vegans saturate faster.
- **"Decorative" floor:** below ~3 g/day physiologically unlikely to meaningfully raise muscle creatine / deliver studied benefits for most people. <3 g or hidden-in-blend = often sub-clinical for performance.

## 3. Forms and bioavailability
- **Monohydrate (Creapure or generic micronized): gold standard.** ~99% bioavailability; +20–40% muscle creatine. Most studied; overwhelming efficacy/safety/tolerability evidence. Powder stable long-term.
- **HCl:** higher solubility; "lower dose / better absorption / GI" claims. Human head-to-head: effective but NOT superior to monohydrate. Limited long-term comparative data.
- **Buffered/alkaline (Kre-Alkalyn):** one direct comparison found NO significant differences vs monohydrate. Superiority largely marketing.
- **Ethyl ester (CEE):** NOT superior (sometimes inferior); converts to creatinine. Limited human data.
- **Citrate/malate/salts/dipeptides:** better solubility sometimes; limited human superiority evidence; often less creatine per gram.
- **Overall:** no alternative has consistent replicated human evidence of superiority over monohydrate (ISSN recommendation).
- **Solubility/stability:** monohydrate moderate solubility; micronized mixes better. Dry powder stable for years. In solution creatine → creatinine (inactive) via cyclization; faster at LOWER pH + HIGHER temp. Acidic beverages (pH 3–4.5) significant loss in days–weeks (e.g., 12–21% in 3 days at 25°C; up to 90% in 45 days acidic RT). Neutral pH + refrigeration slow it.
- **Dairy matrix (food-science/formulation):** creatine unstable in liquid, esp. heat (pasteurization/UHT) + acidic pH — a known RTD functional-dairy formulation challenge. One patent (milk beverage + added protein): initial sterilization drop then gradual degradation, ~56–60% retained after 35 weeks at 21–30°C (~3 g from initial ~5 g in a 330 ml example); protein helps. Without stabilization, significant shelf-life loss expected. Powder or post-pasteurization addition avoids this. Limited broad literature beyond patents + aqueous stability studies.

## 4. Safety
- Excellent established profile at loading (≤20–30 g/day short-term) + maintenance (3–5 g/day), short + long term (5+ yrs, clinical populations infant→elderly). Thousands of trial participants; no consistent adverse renal/hepatic/other effects in healthy individuals. Primary effect = intracellular water weight gain. GRAS noted in some contexts.
- **UL:** NONE established (NIH ODS, EFSA) — because no dose-dependent harm threshold identified up to studied high doses, not for lack of study.
- **Kidney myth:** persistent myth for NORMAL kidney function — no impairment of GFR/renal markers; transient serum-creatinine rise = increased production/turnover, not damage. Caution/contraindication for PRE-EXISTING kidney disease (medical supervision). Case reports often confounded.
- **Contraindications:** pre-existing renal impairment (consult physician); limited pregnancy data; no major drug interactions at standard doses; minors under supervision appear safe but general supplementation not broadly endorsed without need.
- **Defensible consumer statement:** "Creatine monohydrate is one of the most researched dietary supplements available. At typical doses (3–5 g/day) it has a strong safety record in healthy adults across short- and long-term studies, with no evidence of harm to kidney function in people with normal kidneys. Those with pre-existing kidney conditions should consult a healthcare provider before use. The most common effect is modest water retention in muscles."

## 6. Label-honesty / dose-adequacy
- Honest label: states EXACT creatine per serving (e.g., "5 g creatine monohydrate") at effective dose (3–5 g), specifies form (monohydrate). Avoids burying in "proprietary blends"/"matrices". Third-party testing (NSF, Informed-Sport) adds credibility.
- "Fairy-dusted": lists creatine/blend but delivers trivial amounts (<3 g, often ≤1 g) or hides dosing. More common in multi-ingredient pre-workouts/stacks than pure creatine. Known practice to list trendy ingredients sub-clinically for label appeal.
- **Verification standard:** standalone "Creatine Monohydrate – X g per serving" (X ≥ 3 g, ideally 5 g), no proprietary-blend obfuscation.

## 7. Creatine in functional dairy / protein drinks
- Appears in some protein-fortified dairy drinks/yogurts (**[report cites Tnuva GO — UNVERIFIED, conflicts with Shufersal scrape which found Tnuva GO = collagen; creatine SKUs found were Yoplait GO, 0 disclosed dose]**; similar international RTD). Typical claimed/inferred doses ~1–5 g/serving; many RTD use lower/token (<3 g effective) for cost/taste/stability.
- Doses frequently short of / at low end of effective 3–5 g range, esp. with stability losses. Partial benefit / marketing value; often sub-clinical for full saturation unless multiple servings.
- **Co-formulation:** pasteurization/UHT accelerates degradation; pH + protein/sugar interactions affect stability/solubility; protein may improve long-term retention (patent evidence); liquid RTD challenged for consistent full dose over shelf life vs powders added at consumption; some mitigate via formulation/packaging; powdered add-ins or stabilized forms are alternatives.
