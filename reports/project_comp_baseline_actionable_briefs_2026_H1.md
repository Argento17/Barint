## Signal 1 — US Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030

**Corrected one-paragraph brief.**
The Dietary Guidelines for Americans 2025–2030 are **final and enacted**, released jointly by USDA and HHS on **2026-01-07**. They shift toward a higher-protein emphasis (a population intake target of 1.2–1.6 g/kg body weight/day), recommend full-fat dairy with no added sugars, explicitly advise avoiding **"highly processed foods,"** and effectively drop endorsement of seed/vegetable oils in favor of olive oil, butter, and tallow. The guidelines are **US federal nutrition policy only** — they carry no Israeli regulatory authority — and they are partly **contested**: they diverge from their own 2024 Dietary Guidelines Advisory Committee (DGAC) Scientific Report on points including saturated fat, and academic reviewers (Harvard Chan, Stanford, Brown) characterize parts of the protein emphasis as ahead of the evidence. "Highly processed foods" is the DGA's own term and is **not** identical to NOVA ultra-processed (UPF).

- **Allowed Bari use:** Content and research context only — as a US-policy reference point when discussing "highly processed food" framing or protein-quality literacy, **always** attributed as US guidance dated 2026-01-07 and flagged as diverging from its own scientific committee.
- **Out-of-scope Bari use:** Any scoring change or threshold; presenting the 1.2–1.6 g/kg target as a per-product Bari rule; equating "highly processed foods" with Bari's NOVA/UPF treatment; implying Israeli regulatory force; any Israeli-shelf consumer claim without Bari scrape evidence.
- **Source row IDs:** Register Signal 1 (USDA/HHS DGA 2025–2030; dietaryguidelines.gov / realfood.gov).
- **Verdict:** **PASS**

## Signal 3 — Protein trend / "protein-washing" risk

**Corrected one-paragraph brief.**
Protein is a genuinely dominant front-of-pack claim in the US market: trade and retail analyses (Mintel; 84.51°/Kroger; Cargill; Marketing Brew, 2025) report that the large majority of highest-velocity SKUs carry front-pack protein claims and that roughly half of shoppers intend to increase protein intake. The associated **"protein-washing"** concern — products carrying prominent high-protein labels while adding little real nutritional value and often being heavily processed or overpriced — is a **marketing/health-halo observation consistently reported across credible trade press, not a clinical or regulatory finding**, and "protein-washing" is a coined term, not a standardized definition. This maps directly to Bari's anti-deception mission (a front-of-pack claim that can mislead), but the underlying evidence is **commercial/US-centric**.

- **Allowed Bari use:** Content (educating that a high-protein claim ≠ a healthy product) and research (characterizing, from Bari BSIP0/BSIP1 scrape data, how protein claims co-occur with high sodium/sugar/UPF in scored Israeli categories) — with "protein-washing" labeled as a marketing observation.
- **Out-of-scope Bari use:** Any new "protein penalty" or scoring/methodology rule; presenting "protein-washing" as a clinical/regulatory finding; Israeli-shelf consumer claims sourced from US trade reports rather than Bari scrape evidence.
- **Source row IDs:** Register Signal 3 (Mintel; Marketing Brew 2025-10-02; 84.51°; Cargill, 2025).
- **Verdict:** **PASS WITH FIXES** — usable once "protein-washing" is framed as a marketing/health-halo observation (not a defined clinical risk) and any Israeli consumer claim is backed by Bari scrape data rather than US trade figures.

## Signal 5 — "Glucose Goddess" misinformation

**Corrected one-paragraph brief.**
The "Glucose Goddess" (Jessie Inchauspé) phenomenon is a real, professionally-contested case of consumer glucose/sugar misinformation. **Provenance correction (must preserve):** Channel 4 (UK) **aired the show** (*Eat Smart: Secrets of the Glucose Goddess*, 2025); it was **not** a Channel 4 *Dispatches* investigation or a Channel 4 "debunking." The **debunking comes from an external clinician coalition — the Freedom Food Alliance open letter (June 2025), signed by 40+ RDs/nutritionists/clinicians** — plus dietitian commentary. Critics argue the content oversimplifies physiology by urging non-diabetics to minimize normal, healthy glucose spikes, relies on anecdotal/personal-CGM data, and overstates links (e.g., to acne). Inchauspé has a biochemistry background and is not an RD/clinician, and her claims are **media/misinformation, not scientific evidence** (the underlying glucose-hack claims rate Insufficient/Weak).

- **Allowed Bari use:** Content (only) and research — as a labeled example of why front-of-pack "blood-sugar-friendly"/spike framing is unreliable, with the debunking attributed to the Freedom Food Alliance/clinicians and all underlying claims labeled Insufficient-evidence misinformation.
- **Out-of-scope Bari use:** Citing Inchauspé's claims (or the show) as science; attributing the debunking to Channel 4 or to a "Dispatches" investigation; any scoring or product implication; any Israeli-shelf consumer claim without Bari scrape evidence.
- **Source row IDs:** Register Signal 5a (Channel 4 press/series page) + 5b (Freedom Food Alliance open letter, June 2025).
- **Verdict:** **PASS WITH FIXES** — usable only with the corrected attribution (Channel 4 aired; Freedom Food Alliance debunked) and strict media/misinformation labeling.
