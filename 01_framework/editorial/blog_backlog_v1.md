## BL-001 🟡 "GLP-1 Friendly" — the label that promises nothing
**Source signal:** Hebrew Health Scan radar (2026-06-23) → NPR (Mar 2026, still circulating).
**Researched & verified:** 2026-06-24 (Research Agent evidence brief).

**Angle.** "GLP-1 Friendly" / "GLP-1 Companion" food labels are proliferating with **zero
regulatory definition** anywhere — US (FDA/USDA) or Israel (MoH). Any brand can put the
phrase on any product with no minimum protein, fiber, or calorie threshold. It looks
clinical; it enforces nothing.

**Why it's Bari's lane.** Textbook label-noise vs. clarity ([[bari_brand_anchor_clarity]]).
The consumer here is doubly vulnerable — on a powerful drug AND being sold a clinical-
sounding term that means whatever the marketer wants.

**Verified evidence spine (all sources retrievable):**
- Zero regulatory definition confirmed by independent, non-trade sources: National
  Agricultural Law Center (Jul 2024); University of Arkansas Extension (Mar 11 2026,
  nutrition specialist Lida Araghi: "not a standardized or regulated nutrition claim").
- On-pack adoption is real and dated: Conagra Healthy Choice "On Track" badge (Jan 2025,
  26 products); Nestlé Vital Pursuit; Mission Carb Balance. UK retail followed (Morrisons
  "Small & Balanced", M&S "Nutrient Dense", Co-op "Good Fuel", Jan 2026).
- USDA "review" of Conagra's badge = compliance with *existing* protein/fiber/calorie
  claim rules, NOT recognition of "GLP-1 friendly" as a defined category. Important nuance.
- Real clinical logic exists (protein → muscle retention, fiber → GLP-1 gut-motility
  slowdown, smaller portions → reduced appetite) — but the label does not guarantee any
  of it is present at meaningful levels.

**IL status / why 🟡:** Not yet observed on Israeli shelves (US/UK only so far). UK adopted
~12-18 months after the US, so a ~12-24 month IL arrival is plausible but speculative. Hold
as a watch item; promote to 🟢 the moment it appears in an Israeli retailer.

**Honest consumer takeaway (draft, not approved copy):** The label adds nothing the
nutrition panel doesn't already tell you — read protein/100g, fiber/100g, and calorie
density yourself; the badge just charges a premium for the framing.

**Caveats to preserve in any copy:** don't overclaim Israeli prevalence (none yet); the
clinical logic is genuine (don't dismiss the *needs*, only the *label*); cite the
regulatory-absence to the ag-law-center / Extension sources, not to a brand or trade page.
