10 Technology Leaps for the Comparison-Page Chain

1
DATA TRUST

Dual-Extractor Consensus — vision-LLM reads every label, parser reads every page, disagreement = automatic flag

Today one brittle HTML parser is the single source of truth — which is how website disclaimer text became “ingredients” on 67 products and nothing noticed. Run every product through two independent extractors: the parser, plus a vision model reading the label image with structured output and per-field confidence. Diff them. Agreement = high confidence; disagreement = quarantined before BSIP1 ever sees it.

Kills: the entire “parse corruption discovered at red-team” failure class — at the source.
2
DATA TRUST

Property-Based Testing for the Scoring Engine — invariants, not golden products

The regression suite is 12 hand-picked products. Instead, generate thousands of synthetic products (Hypothesis-style) and assert monotonicity invariants: adding sugar never raises a score · adding an additive never raises a score · removing a data field never raises a score · keyword repetition never raises a score.

Kills: the uncapped fermentation bonus and absence-as-zero would both have been caught mechanically — they are invariant violations, not edge cases. This is how you trust an engine you keep changing.
3
DATA TRUST

Shadow Scoring — every engine change auto-backtests against the full historical corpus

Like a quant-fund backtest: any engine diff runs all ~400 scored products across all categories in shadow and emits a per-product diff report with attribution — which rule moved which score, by how much. Nutrition signs off on a complete impact picture instead of a 12-product sample.

Kills: “did this touch a frozen category?” as a manual check — it becomes a generated table on every change.
4
SELF-MAINTAINING CORPUS

Continuous Scrape with Change Detection — data freshness becomes event-driven

Replace one-shot scrape campaigns (gated on someone flipping a VPN) with a scheduled crawler that content-hashes every product page. A hash change — reformulation, new label, delisting — triggers re-extraction and re-score of that product only.

Bonus: “Bari detects a reformulation before the consumer does” is itself a marketable claim. Freshness stops being a policy and becomes a property of the system.
5
SELF-MAINTAINING CORPUS

Embedding-Based Product Identity Graph — misroutes and duplicates caught by geometry

Embed every product (name + ingredients) and maintain per-category centroids. The granola that sat in the yogurt corpus was an outlier in embedding space — detectable in milliseconds, no human review. The same layer gives cross-retailer product matching, duplicate detection, and “this product doesn’t belong on this shelf” alerts at ingestion.

Kills: category misroutes (the cereal-in-yogurt class) and silent corpus duplication.
6
MACHINE GATES

Claim-Entailment Verification — copy can’t say what the trace didn’t fire, enforced by machine

The deepest copy risk isn’t style, it’s fabrication (“official food source” shipped live once). Build an NLI-style check into the build: every factual claim in a verdict or insight line must be entailed by that product’s glass-box trace JSON — or the build fails. Combine with the banned-phrase linter.

Kills: the read-every-string gate as human discipline — it becomes a compiler guarantee.
7
THE SPINE

The Pipeline Becomes a Real DAG — typed artifacts, lineage, resume, parallelism

Today the “factory” is a folder of bespoke scripts per category. Declare the stages (scrape → enrich → score → QA → package) in an orchestration framework (Dagster / Prefect, or a lean homegrown runner): every artifact typed and hashed, lineage queryable (“which scrape produced this live score?”), failed stages resumable without full reruns, categories processed in parallel.

Kills: the forgotten factory artifacts — they get emitted automatically because they are the pipeline’s edges. The 6-run regen cycle shrinks to re-running only the stages that changed.
8
THE SPINE

One Queryable Datastore — instead of JSON-file archaeology

Products, observations, runs, traces, copy versions, live deployments — as DuckDB / SQLite tables with real foreign keys. The frontend JSON becomes a generated view, not a hand-built artifact. The live-state manifest, category health dashboard, drift detection, and “what changed between run 5 and 6?” all collapse into one-line SQL queries.

Kills: about half the gap-analysis additions in a single move — they stop being features and become queries.
9
MACHINE GATES

Adversarial Corpus Fuzzing — red-team as a build step, not an event

Codify every attack the Red-Team Agent has ever landed — keyword stuffing, marketing-prose injection, implausible macros, disclaimer contamination, grade-boundary values — into a generator that mutates real products into adversarial variants, then assert the pipeline flags or scores each correctly, on every build.

Kills: re-running old attacks by hand. The human red-team spends its time inventing new attacks — the only thing humans are needed for.
10
THE MOAT

The Glass Box Goes Interactive — a grounded “why?” answerer on every product

The trace JSON is Bari’s most defensible asset, and consumers never see it. Ship a per-product Q&A — “why only B?” · “what’s E414?” · “is this better than X for my kid?” — answered by an LLM strictly grounded in that product’s trace + the methodology docs, with hard refusal outside that scope.

The moat: nobody in the Israeli market — or honestly most of the global market — has explainable food scoring at the individual-product level. This converts internal rigor into the consumer-facing differentiator, and is the reason to build the other nine.
