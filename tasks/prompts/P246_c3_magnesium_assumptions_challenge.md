# P246 / Independent challenge of magnesium scoring assumptions (route: C3)

You are the outside-the-family independent reviewer. A consumer comparison page rates Israeli magnesium
supplements. The owner trusts it but says "I'm not sure I understand the science and the assumptions."
Challenge the assumptions independently and in PLAIN ENGLISH. Evidence + judgment only — you do not build

## The model's assumptions (challenge each — is it sound, overconfident, or wrong?)
1. **Score the magnesium ON THE LABEL (elemental mg), not what's absorbed.** Absorption varies hugely
   by form. Is a label-based score honest/useful, or does it mislead?
2. **"Best value" = most elemental magnesium per shekel.** This puts cheap magnesium OXIDE at the top —
   but oxide is widely cited as ~4% absorbed. Is it defensible to rank the least-absorbed form as best value?
   What is the actual absorbed-magnesium picture (oxide vs citrate vs glycinate)?
3. **Citrate ranked above glycinate/bisglycinate** ("citrate has the stronger direct human comparative
   evidence; glycinate is tolerability-forward, not the proven absorption winner"). Is that fair to glycinate,
   or is the citrate-vs-glycinate evidence actually a toss-up?
4. **Minimum effective-dose thresholds: 100 mg (general), 200 mg (sleep), 400 mg (migraine).** A product
   below ~50% of its threshold is treated as ineffective. Where do these numbers come from? Defensible or arbitrary?
5. **A cited assay found 58.7% of EU magnesium supplements were outside label tolerance, regardless of price.**
   If labels are often wrong, what is a label-based score actually worth, and how should it be framed honestly?
6. **Evidence tiers:** energy/fatigue & immune = strong (EFSA-authorized); migraine = moderate; sleep & cramps
   = weak/insufficient. Any miscategorized? Is "EFSA-authorized" the same as "good evidence"?
7. **Hard ceilings:** a too-low dose caps at ~49/100 (D); an unsupported claim caps at ~34 (E). Reasonable?

For each assumption: is it SOUND / SHAKY / WRONG, the evidence, and what you'd change. Then: the single
assumption most likely to embarrass the project if a journalist or scientist attacked it, and the one fix
you'd prioritize.
