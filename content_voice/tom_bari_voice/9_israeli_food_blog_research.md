## 1. The landscape (four strands)

### Strand A — Recipe / lifestyle food blogs *(register: warm, personal)*
The mainstream Israeli food-blog scene. Useful for natural, homey Hebrew idiom and
the "what a real kitchen sounds like" register that anchors Bari's situation
openers ("ריח של שבת", "תבנית אינגליש קייק").
- בצק אלים, oogio.net, קרוטית (רחלי קרוט), רותם ליברזון (rotteml.com), קיבוץ גלויות במטבח, גאולה דרך החיטים.
- Curated lists: Finger Food's "11 בלוגי אוכל ישראלים", Blogeristit (יונית צוק) food-blogger hub.
- **Take:** idiom and warmth only. They sell recipes; they don't critique products.

### Strand B — Dietitian / nutrition blogs *(register: clinical, advice-giving)*
Clinical dietitians writing for consumers.
- צביה (zviadiet.co.il), ד"ר טל ברוריה (tal-health.info), דגנית עין בר (einbar.co.il), נטע אמירי, ארגון רופאים לתזונה (pan-il.org).
- **Take:** these are the **anti-model for tone.** They prescribe ("כדאי להפחית", "מומלץ להעדיף") — exactly what Bari does **not** do. Read them to know what to *avoid*: Bari describes and shows, it doesn't counsel.

### Strand C — Consumer-investigative journalism *(register: THE model for Bari)*
Newspaper consumer desks running structured product investigations. **This is the
closest existing register to Tom's voice** and the most valuable strand.
- **TheMarker** — "דגני בוקר: איפה יש יותר סוכר מעוגיות וחטיפים" (consumer desk).
- ynet / mako / Israel Hayom — recurring "בדקנו 10 סוגים" cereal/product tests.
- Haaretz food desk — "מזון מעובד הוא לא בעייתי. מזון אולטרה-מעובד זה כבר סיפור אחר".
- **Take:** see §2 — this strand splits into a good model and a warning.

### Strand D — Product databases & official label guides *(register: reference)*
- Foodiepedia (foodiepedia.co.il), Clalit / Weizmann / gov.il label-reading guides, Wikipedia "תווית מזון".
- **Take:** useful to see how Israelis are *taught* to read a label (shortest-ingredient-list heuristic, red-label rules) — which is exactly the literacy Bari builds on. **But never a data source.**

## 2. The decisive finding: two opposing registers in Strand C

Reading two investigations side by side gave the single most useful calibration.

### ✅ EMULATE — TheMarker cereal investigation
- **Opens from familiar perception**, then pivots to evidence — identical to Tom's arc. (Opens on a Nestlé/Cheerios ad of kids persuading mom → then tests the products.)
- **Methodology-first credibility:** two senior dietitians independently ranked 12 brands from *unlabeled* ingredient lists and landed on the same ranking. (Bari's equivalent = the transparent score/trace.)
- **The "X לא תמיד אומר Y" move, verbatim register:** *"סירופ תירס נשמע כמעט כמו משהו בריאותי, אבל הוא ודומיו הם למעשה תחליף זול וגרוע."* This is exactly Tom's "נקי לא תמיד אומר חזק".
- **Comparative harm-reduction, not shaming:** *"עדיף לאכול דגני בוקר מאשר לדלג על הארוחה."* No parent-blaming.
- **Makes deception visible** by standardizing (sodium → % of daily, portions → 60g) rather than condemning the shopper. = Bari's "per-100g, tied to meaning."
- **Verdict:** this article could almost be a Bari page. It's the external proof that Tom's voice is a *real, credible Israeli register*, not an invention.

### ⛔ AVOID — ynet "מיליארדים משתתפים בניסוי בלי לדעת" / the ultra-processed scare genre
- **Fear-first framing:** readers as unwitting **test subjects** in "ניסוי עצום על מיליארדי בני אדם"; "מחיר בלתי נתפס"; crisis/medical vocabulary.
- **Disempowering + moralizing:** "you're already compromised" mixed with "אל תביאו את זה הביתה."
- **The ynet health genre escalates to risk claims** ("המנות שמעלות סיכון לסרטן", "מקרב אתכם להתקף לב ולסרטן").
- **Verdict:** this is precisely the register Bari's banned-phrase firewall exists to block (מסוכן, גורם לסרטן, אסור, fear). Every one of these would fail `5_banned_phrases_and_claims.md`. Read it only to recognize and reject the move.

> The Haaretz headline "מזון מעובד הוא לא בעייתי. מזון אולטרה-מעובד זה כבר סיפור אחר"
> is itself a textbook Tom move (refuse the blanket villain, draw the real
> distinction) — even within the scare-leaning outlets, the *framing* that works is
> the discriminating one, not the alarmist one.

## 3. Register lessons folded into the fingerprint
1. **Tom's voice is a validated Israeli register** (TheMarker), not a stylistic gamble — raises confidence in `2_voice_fingerprint.md`.
2. **The dividing line is fear vs. discrimination.** Credible Israeli food writing wins by *distinguishing* (processed vs. ultra-processed, clean vs. strong), exactly Bari's three modes. Scare writing loses trust. This reinforces the Critical/Balanced/Positive split and the banned-phrase firewall.
3. **Standardize to make things visible, don't condemn** (per-100g, % daily) — already in the fingerprint; now externally corroborated.
4. **The shortest-ingredient-list heuristic is consumer common knowledge** in Israel (Clalit/Weizmann/gov.il all teach it) — so Bari's "רשימת רכיבים ארוכה" framing reads as familiar literacy, not jargon.
5. **Dietitian register is the anti-voice.** When a Bari draft starts to sound like Strand B (prescribing, "מומלץ"), it has drifted. The voice-match gate (file 7, item 12) catches this.
