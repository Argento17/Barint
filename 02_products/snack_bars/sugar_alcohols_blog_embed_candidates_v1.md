### FDA — Status and jurisdiction note

Multiple direct FDA URL fetches failed (HTTP 404 / 404 from accessdata.fda.gov and fda.gov consumer URLs). Based on verified search-result content, the relevant FDA position is:

- **FDA labeling:** Sugar alcohols may be listed voluntarily on the Nutrition Facts label under Total Carbohydrate. Declaration is MANDATORY only if the label makes a statement about the health effects of sugar alcohols or sugars.
- **Laxative warning:** FDA requires a laxative warning label specifically for products containing **sorbitol or mannitol**. The warning text is "excess consumption may have a laxative effect." This requirement is codified in 21 CFR 101.80 (mannitol) and related provisions.
- **Maltitol:** FDA does NOT have a specific mandatory laxative warning for maltitol equivalent to sorbitol/mannitol — the EU rule (>10% polyols by weight → mandatory warning) is broader and covers maltitol. This is a material jurisdiction difference.

FDA source authority: Interactive Nutrition Facts Label guidance, October 2021 (confirmed to exist as PDF at accessdata.fda.gov; PDF itself returned 404 on direct fetch — server-side issue, not content removal). Cleveland Clinic article (D3, verified accessible) summarizes the FDA sorbitol/mannitol rule accurately in verbatim.

**The FDA is NOT the right anchor for a laxative-warning claim about maltitol in an Israeli-context article. The EU frame is the correct one, and EUFIC (D1) is the right institutional source.**

## Part 3 — Jurisdiction Honesty Note

The Bari article is built on the EU regulatory frame (Reg. (EU) 1169/2011; polyols = 2.4 kcal/g; >10% polyols by weight triggers mandatory laxative warning). Israel aligns with EU food law on polyol labeling, not US FDA rules.

**The EU-frame sources (D1, D2 — EUFIC) are the most defensible anchors.** They are: (a) from the European Food Information Council, which interprets the same EU regulatory body whose rules apply in Israel; (b) updated 2024 / 2020; (c) publicly accessible without paywall; (d) not brand-funded.

**The US-frame sources (D3 Cleveland Clinic, D4 Harvard Health, FDA) are credible consumer corroboration but must not be cited as the regulatory authority for the >10% laxative warning rule.** The FDA requires laxative warnings only for sorbitol and mannitol specifically, and only if present above a threshold amount — it does NOT require a blanket >10% polyols warning as EU law does. Citing FDA for the Israeli context would be a jurisdiction error. These US sources can add consumer-authority weight to the "GI effects exist" claim but should never be cited for the regulatory threshold.

**For the video embed:** Channel and regulatory authority are separate. The V1 video (Let Me Learn Ya, RD-authored) does not need to cite EU law to be embeddable — the video explains consumer-facing sugar-alcohol biology. The Bari article's EU regulatory frame stands independently in the text; the video supports the "what this means for you" layer.
