01 — Executive Summary

What Bari is, in one breath

Bari turns the noise of a supermarket shelf into a single, defensible verdict. For any food or supplement category it answers the only question a shopper actually has: “of the things in front of me, which is genuinely better — and why?”

The gap we close

A shopper today has two bad options. Public databases and crowd apps are unreliable — wrong barcodes, missing fields, contributed errors. Brand marketing is, by design, not neutral. Israel's own government decided this matters: since 2020 it has mandated red warning labels on high-sugar, -salt and -fat products. But that signal is binary — a product either has a red label or it doesn't. Nobody tells an Israeli shopper, in Hebrew, which of two products is better and by how much. That continuous, explained, category-relative verdict is the whole of what Bari does.

Why it's credible

  • Deterministic engine. Scores come from auditable code, not an AI guessing — every score reproduces exactly from its own record.
  • Only verified data. If a value wasn't read directly off the real product, the field is left empty and the page says so. We never fill gaps with guesses.
  • It isn't generous. Across 499 products only 6% earn an A or better and 56% land at D or E. The engine grades honestly — it is not a marketing tool.

Why it scales

  • A factory, not a craft. Each category is the output of one repeatable pipeline — not a hand-built page.
  • One engine, every category. Improve a rule once and all categories re-flow through the same machinery.
  • Hebrew-native. The whole stack reasons over Hebrew ingredient text — a real, defensible moat in this market.
Positioning

“Bari is the category-relative, evidence-graded shelf for Israeli food and supplements — the only place a shopper gets an honest verdict, with the reason shown, in their own language.”

03 — How It Works

How a product becomes a score

Every product moves through three stages — extract → understand → judge. Real retail data in; an individual, defensible verdict out. The judging stage never sees an outside database; it only sees what was directly captured.

Figure 1 — The pipeline. The engine never reads an external database; it only sees what the scrape captured.

Extract

A four-step scraper pulls product pages from up to four Israeli chains; an OCR step reads physical label photos. The output is raw, per-product observations. If a field isn't found, it stays empty — there is no imputation anywhere in the system.

Understand

Software reads the Hebrew ingredient list — white vs. whole-grain flour, fermentation, additives and how many, the protein source, the food structure — and writes one clean, canonical record per product, tagged with how confident it is.

Judge

The scoring engine turns that record into a score and grade and writes a full reasoning record alongside it. The website only ever displays this output, so the number on screen always traces back to the record that produced it.

The important separation

AI helps read messy Hebrew labels and write the consumer copy. It never sets a nutrition value or a score — that is done by deterministic code. This is exactly what makes Bari's output reproducible and defensible, where an “AI nutrition app” is not.

04 — The Engine

How a score is computed

The engine reads a product across ten dimensions, then runs four discipline steps whose entire job is to make sure no single problem is counted twice. It is plain, deterministic code — same product, same score, every time.

The ten dimensions (each 0–100)

DimensionWeight
Processing quality15%
Nutrient density15%
Calorie density15%
Glycemic quality12%
Protein quality10%
Additive quality10%
Fat quality8%
Satiety support6%
Regulatory quality (red labels)5%
Whole-food integrity4%

Then four discipline steps

“Don't ten dimensions double-count?” — the most important question, answered

It's the right thing to ask: a red label is triggered by sugar, salt or fat — which also live in the glycemic, regulatory and fat dimensions. Two deliberate design choices prevent double-counting. First, weighting: the overlapping dimensions are intentionally light — regulatory quality is only 5%, whole-food integrity 4% — so they nudge, they don't dominate. Second, a de-duplication step exists for exactly this: when several rules trace back to one root cause, the engine keeps the primary signal at full weight and demotes the echoes, with a cap per root cause. Worked example: a sugary cereal loses points on glycemic quality — its primary flaw — and would also trip the red-label dimension. The de-duplication step recognises both trace to sugar, keeps the glycemic penalty in full, and shrinks the regulatory echo to a fraction. Sugar is paid for once, not twice. (We're happy to walk a technical partner through the implementation under NDA.)

Every product gets its own score — and they genuinely differ

Scoring is per-product, not per-category. The chart on the next page shows it: within a single category, products span a wide range — yellow cheeses run from 23 to 87, milks from 34 to 85. The engine separates the genuinely better product from the worse one on the same shelf; it does not hand a category one verdict.

05 — Why It Can Be Trusted

Why the numbers can be trusted

A score is only as good as the discipline behind it. Three rules exist so that any verdict can be defended publicly — to a shopper, a brand, a regulator, or a journalist. And the proof is in the spread: the engine differentiates, and it isn't generous.

1 · Only verified data

The only source is the product itself, read directly. Crowd-sourced databases are never used — not for nutrition, ingredients, names, or images. If we didn't capture it, the page says “data could not be retrieved.” Unknown is acceptable; a guess is not.

2 · Every claim is sourced

Every scoring rule and every consumer claim is tied to a recorded source. Rules can't quietly accumulate — each must be justified, scoped, and reversible. Vague provenance is rejected before anything ships.

3 · Nothing invented

The system never fabricates a product, a nutrition value, or an ingredient. A product with missing data is dropped, not guessed. Honesty over coverage — a smaller true shelf beats a larger fake one.

06 — How It's Built

How it's built

Bari is two clean halves that meet at one place. A data & scoring workshop does all the thinking and produces finished, frozen results; a website simply displays them. Because the two only meet through finished files, the published number can never drift from the reasoning that produced it.

The workshop — where the thinking happens

A collection of automated tools that gather and judge product data. In order:

  • Collectors visit retailer websites and read product pages; a second tool reads photographs of physical labels.
  • A Hebrew-label reader interprets the ingredient list — flour type, additives, fermentation, protein source.
  • The scoring engine applies the ten dimensions and discipline steps and writes a finished result for each product.
  • Automatic tests re-check the work so a change can't quietly break a past result.

The website — where shoppers see it

A fast, modern, mobile-first Hebrew site built right-to-left. It is deliberately “simple-minded”:

  • It reads the finished results and displays them — nothing more.
  • It never re-calculates, re-sorts or re-interprets a score; the ranking is decided upstream.
  • Every page follows one shared visual template, checked for contrast and Hebrew correctness.
  • Adding or updating a category means re-running the workshop, not editing pages by hand.
Why this design is the asset

Because all the intelligence is finished and frozen before the website sees it, three things follow: the site can never show a number that disagrees with its own reasoning; the engine and the website can be improved independently; and a whole category re-generates with a single command, not by re-doing work. That is what makes Bari a platform, not a pile of pages — and what makes new categories cheap to add.

Under the hood, for completeness: the workshop is Python; the website is a modern React/Next.js application in TypeScript. The two communicate through generated data files — the single, deliberate seam between them.

07 — The Team

The team that produces it

Bari runs as a small studio of specialists — a set of focused roles, each owning one job, coordinated by a single lead and held to account by an independent challenger. Data collection, research, content and frontend work are AI-assisted, which is how a lean operation produces a national-scale catalogue — but the scoring logic itself is deterministic code, not an AI judgment.

Data

Runs the collection and scoring pipeline — scrapes the shelves, manages the corpus, produces the scored results for each category.

Nutrition

Owns the scoring philosophy and food-science reasoning — what each dimension means, how categories are interpreted, why a product scores as it does.

Research

Gathers the evidence behind every rule and claim — scientific literature, supplement evidence, competitor and market analysis. Produces facts; doesn't decide.

Product

Makes the strategic calls — what to build, what to cut, which category is next, and the final go / no-go before a category goes live.

Content

Writes the consumer-facing words — the verdicts and explanations — in a consistent, honest, human Hebrew voice.

Frontend & Design

Builds and visually polices the website — implementing pages and enforcing one consistent, accessible, correct Hebrew layout.

Marketing

Owns growth once a category is live — search visibility, content, audience and distribution. (The engine of the go-to-market plan.)

Independent QA / Challenger

The independent challenger. Tries to break every score and every claim before launch — can a verdict be defended in public? It raises problems; it cannot approve its own fixes.

Lead / Coordinator

The founder's operational role: coordinates all of the above off a single shared task list — assigns the work, checks each result against the actual evidence, and is the only role that can mark a job “done.” No “trust me, it's finished.”

The rule that protects trust

Nothing a shopper will read ships without two independent sign-offs — the Content role and the independent challenger. Every consumer sentence, every category, every time.

08 — Market & Timing

The market & why now

Three forces line up behind Bari: a government that has already made nutrition transparency the law, a fast-growing health-and-supplements market, and a shift in how people find trustworthy answers online. Bari is built precisely for this moment.

1 · The state already validated the problem

Since 2020 Israel mandates red warning labels on products high in sugar, salt or saturated fat — a policy the Health Ministry projects will cut consumption ~30%, prevent ~2,200 deaths a year and save ~₪600M annually. The state has decided point-of-purchase nutrition guidance matters. But its signal is binary. Bari is the continuous, comparative layer on top of it — not “does this have a red label,” but “which of these is actually better, and by how much.”

2 · A growing, health-conscious market

Supplements are the second-largest over-the-counter category in Israel after painkillers, and the vitamins & minerals market alone is projected to approach US$0.5 billion by 2029, growing ~5% a year. Consumers are actively shifting to health-conscious buying — and increasingly shopping online, where a comparison layer is most useful.

3 · The search shift — why credibility wins now

In 2026, AI-generated answers sit above search results and absorb the clicks. The sites that still win are the ones AI cites — and citation now flows to original data and credentialed authorship (the E-E-A-T signals search engines now reward). Bari is exactly that profile: a proprietary 499-product dataset, plain honest verdicts, and — with the right partner — a licensed clinical authority behind the methodology. The integrity discipline isn't overhead; in this search era it is the growth strategy.

10 — Go-To-Market

The plan: five phases

A deliberate sequence. Each phase earns the right to the next: trust builds audience, audience builds an irreplaceable dataset, the dataset opens institutional doors, institutions justify funding, and funding opens new markets and models. Revenue stays modest and clean early — credibility is the asset we are compounding.

1

Audience, traction & credibility

Win attention and trust. Comparison pages are durable assets that answer “which X is healthier” for years. The 2026 playbook: rank by being cite-worthy — original data and a credentialed authority — so AI answers reference Bari; own the Hebrew long-tail of category questions; build a newsletter as a direct, owned audience; grow on YouTube/Reddit-style channels where AI citations concentrate. Monetisation here is light and non-intrusive: modest referral and sponsorship, never over the top, never touching a score. In parallel, in the pipeline: begin a personalised consumer engine that turns the scored catalogue into “best for you” given goals and restrictions — the feature that converts visitors into a returning, logged-in audience.

2

Become the trusted database — and sell it where it's needed

The audience phase produces the real moat: a clean, verified, continuously-scored national food & supplement dataset. That asset is valuable to the institutions that lack it. Health funds (Kupot Holim): all four run advanced member apps — several already with nutrition and healthy-lifestyle features — and serve the whole population. Bari can power an embedded “what should I actually buy” layer inside those apps (e.g. a Clalit or Maccabi member scanning a shelf and getting a trusted verdict). Government & public health: a natural complement to the red-label reform — the continuous, comparative data the binary label can't provide. Retail chains: private-label benchmarking and a healthier-shelf signal. This is where meaningful, defensible B2B revenue begins.

Why phases 1 → 2 are one motion

You can't sell a “trusted national database” to a health fund until the public already treats you as trustworthy. Phase 1 manufactures exactly the credibility and the dataset that make Phase 2 a credible conversation. They are not two businesses — they are one compounding loop.

10 — Go-To-Market (cont.)

Phases 3–5, and the flywheel

3

Clinical legitimacy — the licensed-professional path

Dietitians in Israel are a regulated, licensed profession (Ministry of Health registration and exam). Evaluating Bari's fit with a licensed clinical authority — for methodology sign-off and professional-facing tools — converts trust into formal credibility. It strengthens the institutional sale in Phase 2, satisfies the “credentialed author” signal that wins modern search, and opens a professional channel (clinics, dietitians citing and using Bari). This is the most direct point of overlap with a nutritionist partner.

4

Funding & international expansion

With audience, an institutional dataset business, and clinical legitimacy, Bari is fundable. The same engine is country-agnostic — the moat is the local label data and language, exactly the part Bari already proved it can build. Capital funds replication into new markets, starting where regulatory tailwinds (front-of-pack labelling) and health-conscious demand mirror Israel's.

5

Diversify into commerce

Once Bari is where people decide what to buy, helping them buy it is the natural next step — contextual, opt-in commerce: “add the better option to your cart,” healthier-basket building, and curated marketplace links, all kept strictly downstream of and walled off from the score. A monetisation of intent, not of influence.
